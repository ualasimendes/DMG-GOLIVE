# Humanizer: Clean AI Writing Patterns

Rewrite text that sounds AI-generated so it reads naturally, authentically, and clearly. Keep the writer's facts, meaning, and voice while eliminating robotic tropes.

Based on Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing* (maintained by WikiProject AI Cleanup).

## Core Rules

1. **Find AI patterns:** Audit the text against the known patterns below.
2. **Keep every claim:** Shorten dull parts and tighten structure, but preserve every factual claim.
3. **NEVER invent facts:** Do not invent dates, names, metrics, quotes, or sources. If a detail is missing, ask or simplify.
4. **Match the author's voice:** If the user supplies a writing sample, prioritize matching their sentence rhythm, vocabulary, and quirks over strict defaults.

## Key Patterns to Eliminate

### 1. Inflated Claims & Melodrama
- **Watch out for:** *"stands as a testament"*, *"a pivotal moment"*, *"evolving landscape"*, *"indelible mark"*, *"vital role"*, *"shaping the future of"*, *"deeply rooted"*.
- **Fix:** State what happened directly without puffery.

### 2. Promotional & Sales Clichés
- **Watch out for:** *"nestled in the heart of"*, *"boasts a"*, *"vibrant tapestry"*, *"breathtaking"*, *"groundbreaking"*, *"must-visit"*, *"delve into"*, *"foster a deep connection"*.
- **Fix:** Use plain descriptive nouns and verbs.

### 3. Superficial "-ing" Clauses
- **Watch out for:** Appending weak participial phrases to make mundane facts sound profound (*"The institute was opened in 2012, highlighting the region's commitment to innovation and underscoring its pivotal role..."*).
- **Fix:** Break into clear independent statements or omit the empty filler.

### 4. Overused AI Vocabulary
- **High-frequency AI words:** *delve, tapestry, crucial, pivotal, enhance, foster, interplay, intricate, showcase, underscore, align with, garner, bolster, seamlessly*.
- **Fix:** Replace with simpler, direct terms (*explore, mix, important, key, improve, build, show, match*).

### 5. Formulaic Syntax Traps
- **"Not only X, but Y" / "It's not just X, it's Y":** Overused by LLMs for dramatic contrast.
- **Forced Rule of Three:** Packing sentences with arbitrary triads (*"delivers speed, reliability, and security"*).
- **Tail Negations:** Adding clipped endings (*"The options appear automatically, no guessing needed"* → *"The options appear automatically."*).
- **Em-dash (—) overload:** Moderate excessive em-dash interruptions with periods, commas, or parentheses.
- **Vague Weasel Attribution:** *"Experts argue"*, *"Industry observers noted"* without naming real sources.
